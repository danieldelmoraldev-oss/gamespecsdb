The Switch 2 can display up to 120 frames per second, both on its own screen and on a TV. Very few games use it so far, and seeing it on a TV needs the right setup. Here is what you need to know.

## What the hardware can do

| Mode | 120 Hz support |
| --- | --- |
| Handheld (built-in screen) | Yes, at 1080p |
| TV mode | Yes, at up to 1440p, on a TV that supports 120 Hz |

A game has to be made to run at 120 FPS. The console does not raise the frame rate of games on its own.

## Games that run at 120 FPS

In the GameSpecsDB database, four games have a sourced 120 FPS mode in October 2026:

| Game | How it reaches 120 FPS |
| --- | --- |
| [Metroid Prime 4: Beyond](/games/metroid-prime-4-beyond-nintendo-switch-2-edition/) | Performance mode: 1080p at 120 FPS on a TV, 720p at 120 FPS handheld. Quality mode runs at 4K or 1080p at 60 FPS. |
| [Hades II](/games/hades-ii-nintendo-switch-2-edition/) | 1080p at 120 FPS in TV mode; 60 FPS in handheld mode |
| [Hollow Knight: Silksong](/games/hollow-knight-silksong-nintendo-switch-2-edition/) | 120 Hz mode on a 120 Hz TV, at a reduced resolution |
| [Ys X: Proud Nordics](/games/ys-x-proud-nordics/) | Optional Performance mode at 120 FPS; 60 FPS by default |

Out of 55 games with a sourced frame rate, that is fewer than one in ten. Most Switch 2 games target 60 FPS, and many larger ones 30 FPS. See the [full 120 FPS list](/lists/120fps-games/) and the [60 FPS list](/lists/60fps-games/), which are updated as new figures are published.

## What you need on a TV

1. **A TV or monitor that supports 120 Hz** through HDMI. Many recent mid-range and high-end TVs do, but often only on some HDMI ports, usually marked "4K 120" or "HDMI 2.1".
2. **The right port and cable.** Use the 120 Hz-capable port and the HDMI cable that came with the console or a certified high-speed one.
3. **The console set to 120 Hz.** Check the TV output settings in System Settings.
4. **The game's 120 FPS mode turned on**, if it has a separate mode, as in Metroid Prime 4 and Ys X.

If any of these is missing, the game falls back to 60 FPS. In handheld mode none of this applies: the built-in screen supports 120 Hz on its own.

## Is 120 FPS worth it?

It depends on the game:

- **Fast action games and shooters** benefit the most: motion looks clearer and controls feel more responsive. Metroid Prime 4 is the best example on Switch 2.
- **Slower games** gain little, and the 120 FPS mode usually costs resolution. In Metroid Prime 4, Performance mode drops from 4K to 1080p on a TV.
- **Battery life** in handheld mode can be shorter when a game runs at a higher frame rate.

If your TV only supports 60 Hz, choose the mode with the higher resolution: you will not see the extra frames anyway.

## 120 FPS vs frame generation

Some games show a smoother image than they actually render, using frame generation. That is not the same as running at 120 FPS: it does not make the game respond faster to your input. The games in the table above render their frames for real. More about this in [TV mode vs handheld performance](/guides/switch-2-tv-mode-vs-handheld-performance/).

## Common questions

**Can Switch 1 games run at 120 FPS on Switch 2?** Only if they get a Switch 2 update or Edition that adds it. Hades II and Hollow Knight: Silksong are examples of Switch 2 Editions that do.
