A Switch 2 box can hold three very different things: a cartridge with the whole game, a cartridge that only unlocks a download, or just a code. They look similar on a store shelf, so here is how to tell them apart before you pay.

## The three formats

| Format | What is in the box | Download needed | Storage used | Resale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full cartridge | The whole game on the card | Only updates | Updates and saves | Yes |
| [Game-Key Card](/guides/what-is-a-game-key-card/) | A card that unlocks the download | The full game | Full game size | Yes |
| Code in box | A download code, no card | The full game | Full game size | No |

## 1. Look at the front of the box

Game-Key Card releases carry a Game-Key Card mark on the front of the box. If you see it, the cartridge does not contain the game.

A full-cartridge box has no such mark. Code-in-box releases are usually marked as containing a download code; check the front and back of the box.

## 2. Check the store listing

Many retailers add "(Game-Key Card)" to the product name. If you buy online, read the full title and the product description: the format is often only mentioned there.

## What to expect by publisher

In our database, Nintendo puts the full game on the cartridge for most of its own new releases, while most big third-party publishers use Game-Key Cards for all of theirs. There are exceptions on both sides: Cyberpunk 2077, Oblivion Remastered, Indiana Jones and the Great Circle, Lies of P and Stellar Blade all ship on full cartridges. The full breakdown is in [which publishers use Game-Key Cards](/guides/which-publishers-use-game-key-cards/).

## Watch out for Switch 1 boxes

A Switch 1 game sold in a regular Switch box usually holds a Switch 1 cartridge, and Switch 1 cartridges work in a Switch 2. Some of these games also get a free Switch 2 update. But a **Nintendo Switch 2 Edition** box is a different product: it is a Switch 2 release, and its format depends on the publisher. Most Switch 2 Editions from Nintendo have the full game on the card; some third-party Editions, such as Civilization VII and Final Fantasy Tactics, come as a download code in the box.

## Does it matter?

It depends on how you play:

- **Slow or capped internet?** Prefer full cartridges. A Game-Key Card can mean a download of 50 GB or more before you can play.
- **Short on storage?** A full cartridge runs from the card and only stores updates. The other two formats need the whole game size free. See [how much storage the Switch 2 has](/guides/how-much-storage-does-switch-2-have/).
- **Buying to resell later?** Full cartridges and Game-Key Cards can both be resold. A redeemed code cannot.
- **Collector?** A full cartridge is the only format that will still be a playable game in the box if the download servers ever close.

## Common questions

**Is a Game-Key Card the same as a digital game?** Not quite. The download and storage are the same, but the card is a physical licence you can lend or sell, and it is not tied to your account.

**Can a game change format later?** A reprint or a later edition can come in a different format, and regions can differ. Check the specific edition you are buying.

**Are full-cartridge games more expensive?** Not always. Prices depend on the publisher and the store, not the format.
