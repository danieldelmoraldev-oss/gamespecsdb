The Switch 2 sets the limits, but each game decides how close it gets to them.

## What the hardware allows

| Mode | Maximum resolution | Maximum refresh rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Handheld (built-in 7.9-inch screen) | 1080p | 120 Hz, with HDR |
| TV mode | 4K at 60 Hz, or 1440p at 120 Hz | 120 Hz |

## What games actually do

Most games run at a higher resolution in TV mode and a lower one in handheld mode, and many keep the same frame rate in both. Some examples from our data:

- **Mario Kart World**: 1440p in TV mode and 1080p in handheld mode, 60 FPS in both.
- **Metroid Prime 4: Beyond** has two presets. Quality runs at 4K and 60 FPS on a TV, and Performance runs at 1080p and 120 FPS.
- **Hades II** runs at 120 FPS in TV mode but 60 FPS in handheld mode.

Many games use **dynamic resolution**: the resolution drops for a moment in busy scenes to keep the frame rate steady. Game pages show a range when that happens, for example "648p–1080p (dynamic)".
