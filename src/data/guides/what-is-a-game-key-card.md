A **Game-Key Card** is a physical Nintendo Switch 2 cartridge that does not contain the game. It contains a licence. When you insert it, the console downloads the full game from the internet to its storage, and from then on the card works as the key that lets you play.

## What it means in practice

- **You need an internet connection the first time.** The whole game has to be downloaded before you can play, and some games are over 20 GB.
- **You need free storage.** The download goes to the internal storage or a microSD Express card, exactly like a digital purchase. Use the [storage calculator](/tools/storage-calculator/) to check it fits.
- **The card must be in the console to play.** It is the proof that you own the game.
- **You can still lend, sell or trade it.** It is a normal cartridge, and it is not tied to your Nintendo Account: the game starts on whichever console the card is inserted into.

## How to tell before you buy

Game-Key Card releases carry a special Game-Key Card mark on the front of the box. Many retailers also add "(Game-Key Card)" to the product name.

Some boxes hold even less: a **code in box** release has no cartridge at all, only a download code. Once you redeem it, the game is tied to your Nintendo Account like any digital purchase, so you cannot lend or resell it.

## How common are Game-Key Cards?

Very common, especially outside Nintendo. Of the 208 Switch 2 games in the GameSpecsDB database in October 2026:

| Physical format | Games | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game-Key Card | 115 | 55% |
| Full game on the cartridge (Switch 2) | 52 | 25% |
| Only sold digitally | 17 | 8% |
| Switch 1 cartridge (Switch 1 games with a free update) | 16 | 8% |
| Download code in the box | 8 | 4% |

The split by publisher is even clearer. Nintendo puts the full game on the cartridge for almost all of its own new releases, while large third-party publishers such as Square Enix, SEGA, CAPCOM, Bandai Namco, Ubisoft and NIS America have released every one of their new Switch 2 games in our database as a Game-Key Card. There are exceptions on both sides, so check the page of the specific game before you buy.

## Why publishers use them

Switch 2 cartridges with enough capacity for a large modern game are expensive to make, and the cost goes up with the size. A Game-Key Card only needs to hold a licence, so the publisher can sell a boxed copy at the same price as the digital one, and stores can still stock it. The trade-off is that you, the buyer, provide the storage and the download.

Size matters here. More than half of the Game-Key Card games we track need over 20 GB, and the biggest ones go well beyond that: Final Fantasy VII Revelation is listed at 130.1 GB, more than half of the console's internal storage. ## Game-Key Card vs full cartridge vs digital

| | Full cartridge | Game-Key Card | Digital |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Playable without a download | Yes | No | No |
| Needs the full game size in free storage | No (only updates) | Yes | Yes |
| Card must be in the console to play | Yes | Yes | No |
| Can be lent or resold | Yes | Yes | No |
| Tied to your Nintendo Account | No | No | Yes |

In short: a Game-Key Card keeps the advantages of a physical game that come from owning an object (lending, reselling, trading in) but loses the ones that come from having the data on the card (no download, no storage needed).

## Common questions

**Can I play a Game-Key Card offline?** After the first download, yes: the card is checked by the console, not by an online server. You need an internet connection only to download the game and its updates.

**What happens if I delete or archive the game?** The card still works as a key. Insert it and the console downloads the game again, so you need an internet connection and the free space once more.

**Is the download the same size as the digital version?** Yes. The card unlocks the same eShop download, so the size on each GameSpecsDB game page applies to both.

**Can I play it on a friend's console?** Yes. Insert the card in their Switch 2, and their console downloads the game to its own storage.
